# Cover Letter — Base + Variants

> This is the master cover letter. Variants below are for specific company types. When tailoring for a specific company, swap in (a) the opening hook with a real reason this company stands out, and (b) at most one project bullet that's genuinely relevant to their stack or domain. Keep the rest stable.

## Why I'm looking (private — informs tone, do not paste into letters)

I'm looking for a role with real ownership — somewhere I can build a product from scratch or take meaningful charge of one in flight, lead its direction, and grow the team around it. I want to step into leadership and product ownership, not just shipping features off a backlog. Company size doesn't matter; what matters is competitive comp and a real growth trajectory.

What energizes me: ambitious work on real-world problems where the impact is visible. I'm a problem-solver at the core — I need to be building things to feel right.

## Base version (~270 words)

> Use this verbatim for most applications. Replace the bracketed bits, keep the rest.

## Variants

### For early-stage startups (Seed–Series B)

Swap the second paragraph for an emphasis on speed and ambiguity:

> The work I'm proudest of started as a 24-hour hackathon prototype: my team and I built the Flee Outpass Portal at IIIT Kottayam (1st of 95+ teams), then matured it into the campus's production system over six months — used daily by 2,000+ students. Doing the prototype-to-production loop on a small team, with no spec handed to you, is the most fun I've had as an engineer. At Shipsy I've kept that pattern at scale: an end-to-end pickup-scheduling system that cut manual ops by 90% across 100+ enterprise accounts, and an LLM-powered auto-config bot that compressed our release cycle from weeks to days.

Close the cover letter with: "I'm comfortable owning ambiguous problems end-to-end and shipping the first version myself. Happy to chat."

### For larger / scaled companies (Series C+ or big tech)

Lead the second paragraph with system-scale work:

> At Shipsy, I architected an end-to-end pickup-scheduling system on MongoDB / NestJS / React, with weekly pre-scheduling, nightly cron automation, and idempotent retry queues — driving a 90% reduction in manual ops and near-zero scheduling errors across 100+ enterprise accounts. I also built a multi-level webhook infrastructure (task / client / customer) with exponential-backoff retries, dead-letter queues, and a real-time observability dashboard, cutting integration failures by 60%. Earlier, the Flee Outpass Portal I built with a small team at IIIT Kottayam (originally a 24-hour hackathon win) is now the institute's production attendance system, used daily by 2,000+ students.

Close with an emphasis on rigor and cross-team work: "I'm comfortable at the system-design level — RBAC/SSO/2FA from zero to production, observability, retries — and enjoy partnering with ops and partner-eng teams to take the friction out of integrations."

### For AI / ML / LLM-tooling companies

Lead with LLM work:

> I've been building with LLMs in production since 2024. At Maximl, I integrated Llama 2 across multi-tenant training products with prompt tuning and an A/B feedback pipeline — driving a 40% engagement uplift and a ~20% lift in prompt accuracy against golden-set benchmarks. At Shipsy, I shipped an LLM-powered auto-config bot inside our configuration platform that compressed our release cycle from weeks to days and drove 70% fewer misconfigurations. Earlier, I built the Flee Outpass Portal — a 24-hour hackathon project (1st of 95+ teams) that became the institute's production attendance system used by 2,000+ students daily.

Close with: "I'm interested in roles where LLM tooling is part of the actual product surface — not bolted on — and where I can own a slice of that end-to-end."

### For product-led / user-facing companies

Lead with user impact:

> The work I'm proudest of is the Flee Outpass Portal — a system 2,000+ students at IIIT Kottayam use every day to enter and exit campus. It started as a 24-hour hackathon project (1st of 95+ teams), and over six months we hardened it into the institute's official attendance system. Building things people actually depend on is what made me want to keep doing this. At Shipsy, I've carried that pattern to enterprise scale: a pickup-scheduling system that cut manual ops by 90% across 100+ accounts, and a configuration platform rewrite with an LLM auto-config bot that turned multi-week releases into multi-day ones.

Close with: "I want to keep building things where the users are visible and the impact is felt."

## Phrases to avoid (carried over from preferences.md)

- "I'm passionate about…"
- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "I'm a self-starter / fast learner / team player"
- "Excited to apply for the role of [X]"
- Buzzword stacks ("scalable, performant, robust, enterprise-grade")
