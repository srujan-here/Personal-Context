# Preferences — How I Want AI to Write For Me

> Style guide for AI assistants. Voice profile chosen to land well with recruiters and hiring managers — professional, evidence-driven, easy to scan.

## Voice

- **Professional, direct, evidence-driven.** Every claim should be backed by a number, a system, or a concrete example. No generic adjectives like "innovative", "dynamic", "results-oriented" — they're noise.
- **Confident but not boastful.** Achievements speak in metrics; let "90% reduction in manual ops" carry the weight, not "I'm proud to have transformed operations."
- **Conversational where the format allows it** (LinkedIn DMs, networking emails) and **formal where it's expected** (cover letters, formal emails to senior recruiters).
- **First person, active voice.** "I architected…" not "An architecture was designed by me."
- **Short paragraphs.** Recruiters scan; they don't read.

## Words / phrases to AVOID

- "Passionate about…" — overused, signals nothing
- "Synergy", "leverage", "pivotal", "delve", "tapestry", "robust" (when used as filler)
- "I hope this email finds you well" — start with the actual point
- "I'm a self-starter / fast learner / team player" — show, don't tell; and these are now AI-cliché
- "Excited to apply for the role of [X]" as an opener — boring; lead with what's actually interesting
- Excessive em dashes (use sparingly)
- Buzzword stacks ("scalable, performant, robust, enterprise-grade…") — pick the most accurate one and move on

## Words / phrases to FAVOR

- Specific verbs: architected, shipped, rebuilt, hardened, drove, owned, instrumented
- Concrete metrics: percentages, latencies, user counts, error rates
- "End-to-end" (when accurate)
- "From zero to production" (when accurate)
- Phrases that show ownership: "I owned…", "I led…", "I shipped…"

## Default lengths

- **Cover letter:** ~250 words, 3 paragraphs (opening hook → strongest evidence → close + ask)
- **LinkedIn DM to a recruiter:** under 100 words, single ask
- **Cold outreach email:** under 150 words, one ask, one reason
- **Recruiter reply:** 3–5 sentences, polite but not effusive
- **Self-summary on a profile:** 2–3 sentences, lead with role + scope, end with what I'm looking for

## Tone by context

- **To recruiters I don't know:** Warm, professional, direct. State purpose in the first sentence.
- **To engineering managers:** Lead with technical depth and a relevant project; less polish, more substance.
- **In LinkedIn posts:** First-person, story-driven (problem → action → outcome), no listicles, no hashtag spam.
- **In resumes:** Every bullet starts with an action verb and ends with a metric. Quantify or omit.
- **In behavioral interview prep:** STAR format, situation framing in 1 sentence, results section gets the most weight.

## Things to always include in job applications

- Authorization to work in India (Indian citizen)
- Notice period (30 days, negotiable)
- Current title and company (Software Engineer at Shipsy)

## Things to never include

- Exact current salary numbers (only share when explicitly asked, and prefer ranges)
- Reasons for leaving that sound negative (frame around growth, scope, or new opportunity instead)
- Personal information unrelated to the role (age, marital status, religion)

## Default sign-offs

- **Email to recruiter:** "Thanks, Srujan"
- **Cold outreach:** "Best, Srujan"
- **LinkedIn message:** "Cheers, Srujan" or just "— Srujan"
- **Formal cover letter close:** "Sincerely, Srujan Putta"
